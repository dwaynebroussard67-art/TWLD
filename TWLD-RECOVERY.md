## B. HOW THE BOOK WORKS — your rules, in your words

Stated 06/19/2026. These are the governing constraints.

**It is not a memoir.** It is the story of Jesus — the silent years, the stretch the Western
church skips, the record the Ethiopian canon still carries. Your life is the mirror held against
it.

**Structure:**
- The Introduction is both of you, together.
- Then it splits. One chapter is you at an age. The next is Jesus at that same age.
- The two threads run separated almost the entire book — "because he was perfect and I wasn't,
  not even close."
- They meet again in a **prison cell** at the end. That is the circle closing.

**The purpose of the pairing:** "to create a contrast between what a perfect life looks like and
what a non-perfect life looks like. It's to hold a mirror up to everybody else."

**The instruction that governs everything else — feeling over action:**
"People never understand what happens. They understand how it feels... concentrate heavily on
the feeling more than the action of the stuff that's happening."

**The hard limit on His interior life:**
"We can't really say what he felt. We can't infer, even assume that we know how he felt. But we
can relate our own experience, our own human experience, in those moments — how we would have
felt. So we have to use a lot of words like *I imagine it felt* ... unless the text directly says
how he felt. I don't want to assume stuff like that. We're not gonna put words in his mouth or
in his head."

**Why you wrote it:** "I know very little about it. I'm just starting to learn about what the
silent years and what was going on... that's part of the reason I wanted to do the book — so I
could learn it, and because people need to know about it."

**Open item since 06/19:** the age map. Each of your chapters needs its age pinned so the Jesus
chapter can be placed beside it. You said you'd have to read back through to supply them. Never
delivered.

## D. THE CHAPTER TWO CORRECTION — settled 08/02/2026

**What the chapter currently says:** He took off His divinity, handed His power back to the
Father in full, became 100% man, and at the cross had so divested He could only beg, not command.

**Why it can't stand:**

1. *Tewahedo* means **made one**. The confession is the union of divine and human without
   separation, confusion, or change. Divinity is never subtracted. "Took off His divinity" doesn't
   land off your sources — it reverses their central nerve.
2. Philippians 2:7 — your own footnote — argues the other way. Historic kenosis is self-emptying
   **by addition**: He emptied Himself by *taking* the form of a servant. Veiled glory, ungrasped
   privilege. Not removed deity.
3. Luke 23:43. "Today you will be with me in Paradise" — spoken *after* the point the chapter says
   He'd laid it all down.
4. "You can't kill God" is answered by the tradition through union, not subtraction: *one of the
   Holy Trinity suffered in the flesh.*

**Your counters and where they landed:**

- *Surrendered, not taken* — **you were right, keep the word.** Surrender is an act of sovereign
  will. It carries weight "took off" doesn't.
- *Knowledge, not authority* — He doesn't grant Paradise, He reads it. Strong move; it neutralized
  23:43 better than most who make that claim. But the knowing doing the work is divine knowledge.
  The deity was kept and renamed "understanding" so it could stay in the room after the chapter
  said it left.
- *The memory analogy* (Claude on your mother's phone is still Claude) — your strongest argument,
  and it proves nature-unchanged / access-altered. Which defends verse 43 and defeats "He had to
  shed divinity so they could kill Him." The same nature can't be present-and-unchanged for the
  promise and absent-enough-to-die eight verses earlier.

**Conceded 08/02. The footing to rewrite on:**

> He didn't surrender His divinity. He surrendered the **use** of it. He kept everything — the
> knowing, the identity, Alpha and Omega intact — and united it to a human nature that could bleed
> and stop and die. Then He refused, as God, with all of it still in His hand, to call the nails
> off. That's how you kill God: not by making Him killable, but by joining Him to flesh that was,
> and watching Him decline to save Himself.

**What survives the swap:** the thief/guards distinction whole, the trust in the Father, the
eye-level intimacy. One sentence changes. Chapter One's Harrowing gets *stronger* — the One who
goes down has to still be God when He arrives or the doors don't come off.

**Second flag, still open — the guards.** Do not write that their sins couldn't be forgiven
because they didn't ask. That makes mercy wait on the sinner and the whole book runs the other
way. Grace-first: He interceded when they couldn't intercede for themselves. The offer goes out
ahead of the asking.

**Third flag.** Luke 23:34a ("Father, forgive them") is textually disputed — absent from several
of the earliest manuscripts, bracketed in critical editions. Keep it if you want, but know it
going in if you're building weight on it.
