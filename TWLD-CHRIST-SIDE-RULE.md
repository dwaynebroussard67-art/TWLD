## 2. RECOMMENDED RULE — THREE TIERS, ONE PAGE

Do not choose between "never invent" and "never imagine." Do both, but label which you're doing.

### Tier 1 — STRICT (core chapters, 90% of the book)
**No invented interiority or speech for Jesus.** You narrate around Him, not through Him. Paraphrase, witness, or cite — but don't write His lines.

- Allowed: "He kept working with His hands. He kept Torah. He waited." (inference from Luke 2:52)
- Allowed: "Luke says He was overwhelmed to the point of death." (citing Matt 26:38)
- Not allowed in Tier 1: "Jesus thought..." / "Jesus said to Adam..." as dramatic dialogue.

This protects you from the deepest criticism — that you put words in the mouth of the One you most want to honor.

### Tier 2 — LABELED IMAGINATIVE INTERLUDES (clearly fenced)
**Invented scenes are allowed only when the page tells the reader it's invention.**

Examples that would move to Tier 2:
- The Thief's-Eyes interlude (Dismas).
- Any future dramatized Harrowing dialogue.
- The potsherd, if you keep it.

Formatting rule: Each Tier 2 section gets a header — *Imaginative Interlude — Ancient Tradition, Offered Devotionally* — and a footnote with its source (e.g., "This scene follows the Gospel of Nicodemus, 4th c., not Ethiopian canon. The splinter and dialogue are the author's addition."). The Decisons §II two-tier sourcing system already approved this move.

This is not a loophole. It's honesty. The Ethiopian tradition you love is *full* of devotional storytelling — it distinguishes what is Scripture from what is story told around the fire about Scripture. Do the same English.

### Tier 3 — DIRECT CITATION (safest ground)
When Jesus speaks, let Him speak in the words we already have: Gospels, 1 Enoch Son-of-Man passages, Psalms He prayed, the Creed line you reclaim. Your strongest line — "Give me one more breath, and I'll breathe for both of us" — is *your* line, not His. It never violates Tier 1, which is why it's protected.

**One-sentence test for every Jesus-side paragraph:** Can a reader tell whether this is Scripture, ancient tradition, or my imagination — without guessing?

If not, we have one edit to make.

## 3. WHY ETHIOPIA, WHY NOW — AND HOW TO SAY IT WITHOUT INSULT

You want to point at the Jesus the West flattened and Africa preserved, without insulting either. That's right — and it's delicate. The best way is to honor both as witnesses, not rivals.

**Do:**
- Name Ethiopia's 81-book Tewahedo canon by name. It *is* Scripture to hundreds of millions of Christians. Using it as your Scripture tier is a theological choice, not a historical claim about where a 19th-century manuscript was forged.
- Say plainly what it does and doesn't contain: it gives you Enoch, Jubilees, Meqabyan — judgment, angelology, the Hidden One before creation — but *no* boyhood or silent-years narrative. That absence is why Luke's silence matters, and why you need inference rather than a lost gospel.
- Credit the West too: the Apostles' Creed line "He descended into hell," the Anastasis icon, and the Harrowing texts you cite (1 Peter, Ephesians, Colossians, Revelation) are Western-carried witnesses. You're not rejecting them; you're reclaiming the Saturday they buried.

**Don't:**
- Claim Ethiopia has a secret life-of-Jesus text covering ages 12–30. It doesn't, and that claim is easily checked. You lose credibility where you most need it.
- Present Enoch/Jubilees as "the Ethiopian canon's story of Jesus's childhood." They aren't. Keep the tiers clean.
- Use "African" as a monolith. Name the tradition: *Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo*, preserved in Ge'ez, read devotionally alongside the Gospels for centuries.

**A reviewer recommendation (from the audit):** Before print, one Tewahedo-informed reader and one broader evangelical/Catholic/Orthodox source editor. Not to censor you — to keep you from being dismissed on a factual footnote when your testimony is true. This is stewardship, not permission.

## 4. DRAFT DISCLAIMER — FOR THE FRONT MATTER (in your voice, edit freely)

This is not legal boilerplate. It's one page, blunt, in the register of "there is nothing good in D."

> **A Note Before You Read**
>
> I am not writing the fifth Gospel. There are four, and they are enough. Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John saw one Man four different ways and told the truth without contradicting each other — because one life can be true and still look different to each witness.
>
> This book is my witness chair. It is Jesus's story as I lived it, as I was told it, and as I have come to understand it after thirty years of being rescued by it. Where I cite Scripture, I will tell you whether I treat it as Scripture or as ancient tradition offered devotionally — including the Ethiopian books that have shaped my understanding. Where I imagine a scene to show you what the tradition describes, I will tell you on the page.
>
> I have tried to be true to Jesus as I know Him. If another follower knows Him differently, that does not make either of us a liar. It makes us human witnesses to a King larger than any one language can hold. My hope is that where I have seen clearly, you will see Him more clearly. Where I have seen through a glass darkly, you will forgive the glass and look for the Man behind it.
>
> Nothing in these pages is invented as fact. Where memory is uncertain I will say so. Where dates conflict in my own telling, I will keep the record of the conflict and give you the truest version I can. What is testimony you can take as testimony. What is interpretation you can take as mine.
>
> I was a misfit. I am still being rescued. This is what love did — and is still doing — to me.
>
> — D.

You can cut or harden any line. Keep the last two sentences. They carry your Romans 7 / "still in process" ethic: not a fixed saint writing back, but a man writing from the desk today.

## 6. ONE MORE PRACTICAL BOUNDARY — VIOLENCE/NEEDLE LANGUAGE

You said: "I don't want to write a book that's full of needles... but I'm not going to shy away from the language... not trying to write a book that fixes anybody... not a junkie book... this is a book of hope."

That maps to a craft rule we can apply in canon:

- **Keep the language.** Your profanity, your South Louisiana cadence, your "I handled it myself" honesty — that's not decoration. It's how your reader knows you're not preaching. We don't soften it into Christian publishing voice.
- **Cut the instruction.** Method-specific self-harm detail and step-by-step Tactics don't make the testimony truer — they make the book unsafe for the exact reader you wrote it for (the one in the pit). The audit flagged this as the one publishing and ministry risk that will get a manuscript sent back. We keep: "the end of myself," "the voices came back," "I was going to close the door from the inside," and the refrain. We remove: the how.
- **Keep the fire, remove the map.** The Taco Bell window scene is stronger as: "I sat at a window at 1 a.m. with a murder in my heart, waiting for a kid who didn't belong. The window stayed up. By mercy, not by me." The reader feels the rot without needing the ransom/mailbox forensic detail to believe you. The same for the bottle/needle — name it, don't choreograph it.

You decide line by line. The principle is: **the book tells the whole truth about what you felt and what you did, without handing the next man a plan to do it.**
