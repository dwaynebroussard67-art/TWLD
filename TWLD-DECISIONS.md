# II. SOURCING — THE CANON QUESTION

**LOCKED, your ruling, verbatim:** *"I want to use and call scripture anything
validated by Ethiopian canon."* You refuse the "apocryphal" label for this
material.

**SETTLED — what that ruling actually gets you.** This was the single most
important correction in the thread and you took it without argument. The 81-book
Tewahedo canon **contains no boyhood or silent-years narrative about Jesus.**
What it actually adds:

- **1 Enoch** — apocalyptic; angels, judgment, the Son of Man. Quoted directly in
  Jude 1:14-15, so it carries New Testament pedigree.
- **Jubilees** — Genesis/Exodus retold with expanded angelology and calendar.
- **1, 2, 3 Meqabyan** — *not* the Maccabees of the Catholic/Orthodox canons.
  Entirely different content. Easy and embarrassing mix-up; don't make it.
- **Sinodos, Book of the Covenant, Ethiopic Clement, Didascalia** — church order,
  liturgy, canon law. **Not narrative. No stories about His life.**

The boyhood texts you want — Infancy Gospel of Thomas, Protoevangelium of James,
Arabic Infancy Gospel — are Greek/Syriac, and were never canonized by any church,
Ethiopia included. Ethiopian communities read some of it devotionally, the way
many ancient communities did. It sits outside their canon too.

**Nothing ancient — Ethiopian, Coptic, Syriac, or otherwise — covers ages 12–30.**
That gap is exactly why Luke calls it a silence.

**SETTLED — the two-tier labeling system.** You use all of it. You label it.
- Scripture tier: Enoch, Jubilees, Meqabyan.
- Ancient tradition, offered devotionally, plainly flagged: everything else.

The argument that won it: this is a much smaller admission than "there is nothing
good in D," which you already put in your own introduction. It costs you nothing
except a claim you don't need.

**SETTLED — the Harrowing source.** *Gospel of Nicodemus* (the *Descensus ad
Inferos*), ~4th century. Not in the Ethiopian canon. It is nonetheless the single
best tool for the finale — it's the text underneath the Anastasis icon and the
Apostles' Creed line, and it's what medieval Christendom used for exactly this
scene. Use it, flag it as ancient tradition, cross-anchor it to the scripture that
authorizes the doctrine.

**SETTLED — hard exclusion.** The *Aquarian Gospel* and the Notovitch "Jesus in
India/Tibet" material are 19th/20th-century inventions. The manuscript Notovitch
claimed never existed. Do not let anything from that family into the book.

**SETTLED — the boyhood-tradition filter.** Only use moments that show restraint,
obedience, or compassion maturing. Some manuscript versions of Thomas contain the
boy Jesus striking a child down; leave those out. Luke 2:52 ("increased in favor
with God and man") is scripture and outranks any tradition text that contradicts
it. This is fidelity, not censorship.

# III. THE SILENT YEARS — HOW TO HANDLE THE GAP

**SETTLED, and it's the smartest structural move in the thread.** Do not invent to
fill ages 12–30. Let the silence be the theological point.

Jesus's chapters in that stretch run **short** — a paragraph or two, built only on
Luke 2:52 and restrained inference (He worked as a *tekton*, He likely buried
Joseph, He kept Torah, He waited). Your chapters in that stretch run **long and
loud**.

The imbalance is not a flaw. It is the argument:

> His silence was obedience. My silence was a wound festering.

Say that once, explicitly, and the structure justifies itself.

# IV. THE THEOLOGICAL CENTER

**LOCKED, yours:** Saturday, not Sunday. Western tradition celebrates the
Resurrection. The actual conquest was the descent. He went into the grave and came
out with the captives.

**LOCKED, yours:** He didn't die *instead of* you going to hell. He died to go
into hell and pull you out. Invasion, not substitution.

**SETTLED — you don't need extracanonical support for this.** The Holy Saturday
argument is already sitting in the Apostles' Creed — "He descended into hell" —
recited every Sunday by churches that never preach it. Plus:
1 Peter 3:18-20 · 1 Peter 4:6 · Ephesians 4:8-10 · Colossians 2:15 ·
Revelation 1:18 · Acts 2:27 / Psalm 16:10 · Matthew 27:52-53.

**SETTLED — put the Anastasis icon in the book.** The standard Eastern Orthodox
resurrection image for over a thousand years doesn't show an empty tomb. It shows
Christ standing on the broken gates of Hades, gripping Adam's wrist, pulling him
out, Eve on the other side. Your climax — *instead of getting Adam, He comes to
get me* — is you stepping into the oldest resurrection image in Christian art.
That's historical weight you get without asking anyone to accept the Ge'ez canon
as authoritative.

**LOCKED — the 33 convergence.** He dies and descends at 33. You were rescued at
33. Play it directly. Build the final movement on the number.

# V. THE BIGGEST STRUCTURAL CHANGE — ONGOING HARROWING

This is the decision that reorganized the whole book, and it came from you.

**LOCKED:** the rescue mission is not a one-time event. Not "I only got this one
chance to get it right." An everyday battle for you, an ongoing rescue mission for
Him.

The shape:
- **Age 9 — the parking lot.** First touch. The suicidal voices stop. Then you
  turn: *if He could fix me that fast, why didn't He help my dad?* You walk away.
  Not atheist — **adversary.** Saved from the bullet, then thirty years trying to
  shoot the King.
- **Age 33 — the cell.** The second meeting. Chains break.
- **After.** You got out, you were doing great, and you stumbled — not because His
  salvation wasn't enough, but because a man can choose to walk away. And you did,
  for a long time.

**LOCKED, and this is the line the book turns on:** rock bottom was *not* what sent
you to prison. Rock bottom was after you got out, after you were saved, after you
knew the truth, and chose not to stay close to Him.

**SETTLED:** that makes this a **sanctification story, not a conversion story.**
Most redemption narratives stop at the cell because that's the satisfying ending.
Yours says the real bottom came after the happy ending. Almost nobody admits that
in print. It is the strongest material in the project. Do not soften it.

**SETTLED — the book does not resolve.** No "and then he was fixed forever." If
the last chapter wraps clean it contradicts everything you confessed.

# VI. PETER, NOT ADAM, IS THE TEMPLATE FOR THE THIRD RESCUE

**SETTLED, and it's the sharpest scriptural find in the thread.**

Adam is the Harrowing scene. Peter is the *structure*. Peter isn't a stranger when
he falls — three years walking with Him, confessed Him as Messiah with his own
mouth, and still denies Him three times, cursing on the third to prove it. Luke
22:62: he went out and wept bitterly. That's rock bottom **after** relationship,
after truth — exactly what you described.

And John 21 is the restoration: the beach, the fire, breakfast. Three questions for
three denials. Each answer ends in a commission — *feed my sheep.* Peter's
restoration is not just forgiveness; it is being handed a job that specifically
involves going after other people.

Which is your line: *the best way to stay out of hell is to pull other people out
of it.*

Build the third-rescue chapter against John 21, not Genesis.

**Supporting anchors, all settled:**
- **Prodigal son (Luke 15:11-32)** — a son with full standing who leaves by choice,
  hits a physical bottom, and the father runs before the apology speech. That's your
  "He lets me, because it's a choice, but every time I call, He comes."
- **Revelation 3:20** — spoken to Laodicea. *Believers.* Already lukewarm. "I stand
  at the door and knock." Present tense, after they've known Him.
- **Luke 15:4-7** — the shepherd doesn't retire after the first sheep. Leaving the
  ninety-nine is a repeatable action, not a one-time headline.
- **James 5:19-20** and **2 Corinthians 1:3-4** — both structured as loops: the
  pulling-out of others is what keeps the puller-out anchored. Not a metaphor you're
  stretching. The text describing the mechanism you live.
- **Lamentations 3:22-23** — written from inside a siege, worst national trauma in
  the Old Testament: mercies *new* every morning. Not rationed, not on a counter.
- **Luke 17:3-4** — seventy times seven. The number is deliberately absurd, designed
  to break the instinct to keep score.
- **Romans 2:4** — it's *kindness* that leads to repentance, not anger. Your "never
  judgmental, never here-we-go-again" stated as doctrine two thousand years ago.
- **Hosea 11** — God as a parent teaching a toddler to walk, Israel bolting back
  into the bondage He pulled them out of, on the record, repeatedly — and "how can I
  give you up." Your exact pattern, put in scripture on purpose.

**SETTLED — Gethsemane is the mirror for your bottoms.** Not just the scripted stop
before the Passion. Christ at His own absolute end — "my soul is overwhelmed to the
point of death" (Matt 26:38), sweating blood (Luke 22:44), asking three times if
there's another way — **and being met there** by an angel sent to strengthen Him
(Luke 22:43). Even He was met at the end of Himself. The meeting didn't remove the
cup. It gave Him strength to drink it.

**SETTLED — the doctrinal landmine you should walk around.** Traditions disagree on
whether salvation can be lost or whether you're describing a believer walking in
disobedience without forfeiting standing. Don't referee a five-century argument
inside your own testimony. Plant the flag on the experiential claim: *I chose to
walk away, I felt the hell of that choice as real as any other hell in this book,
and He came anyway.* Let readers supply their own framework.

# VII. THE PROSPERITY-GOSPEL THESIS — "WHERE'S THE BLOOD AT"

**LOCKED, yours, and you called it the headline above everything else.**

**SETTLED — it moves to the front.** Not buried in Chapter 23. Its own section,
probably Introduction Part III, and reprised right before the Gethsemane/Harrowing
climax so it bookends everything between.

The components, all settled:

**The storm on Galilee (Mark 4:35-41).** Jesus doesn't say "let's see what
happens." He gives a command: *let us go over to the other side.* The storm hits
them squarely **inside obedience**, not outside it. God didn't stop the storm just
because they were on their way to do what He said.

And He's asleep. Not absent — present, and at rest, while they're certain they're
dying. When they wake Him He doesn't apologize for sleeping. He rebukes the wind,
then asks why they were afraid. Which means the story was never about whether the
storm comes. It's about **what your fear exposes about who you believe is in the
boat with you.**

**Romans 7 — Paul is your proof text, not your exception.** This isn't young Saul
before Damascus. This is Paul the apostle, years into ministry, writing to a church
he planted: *the good I want to do, I don't do.* He doesn't resolve it with a
formula. He resolves it with a cry — *wretched man that I am, who will deliver me* —
and then, same breath, *thanks be to God.* Deliverance arrives in the middle of the
confession that the struggle is ongoing. And Romans 8:1 — *no condemnation* — is the
very next verse. Paul puts zero condemnation directly beside an admitted, adult,
apostolic relapse pattern.

**The apostles' track record.** Their real ministry started *after* watching their
friend tortured to death — which means the foundation of the faith was laid by men
who had every reason to conclude God's plan had failed. And tradition holds nearly
all of them were martyred anyway. If the prosperity gospel were true, not one of
them should have died that way. That's not a footnote to the gospel; it's the
gospel's actual track record with the people who served best. **2 Timothy 3:12** —
all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will be persecuted.

**Isaiah 64:6 / Romans 3:23 — the honest mirror.** You drew this line correctly and
it's worth knowing you did: not *question your salvation*, but *question your
relationships* — starting with the one with your Savior, because you may not know
who you're saved by. Isaiah 64:6 used as a **leveler, not a condemner.** Everybody
in the room standing on the same ground. That's the whole design of your Friday
welcome.

**THE ONE CORRECTION TO CARRY.** Don't let this flip into mirror-image prosperity
theology. "Suffering proves you're doing it right" is the same transaction in a
different costume — instead of good things proving God's pleased, bad things do,
and either way His love becomes something you read off your circumstances.

The defensible version, which is what you actually meant:

> **Suffering is the curriculum, not the grade.**

Not a reward, not a punishment — the thing God uses, present tense, to make someone
who can't watch another person drown without jumping in. That's 2 Corinthians 1:3-4
again, and it's a cleaner headline than "if your life isn't hard you're not really
saved," which some readers will otherwise hear.

**Proposed second refrain** (unclaimed — your call whether to keep the wording):

> The storm doesn't mean you heard God wrong. The storm means you heard Him right,
> and the enemy noticed.

The idea being that it does the same structural job as "that's what love does" —
appearing every time suffering shows up in the narrative, so the reader never
mistakes your bottoms for evidence He left.

# VIII. THE REFRAINS

**LOCKED — "and that's what love does."** Working. Earning its place. Gives the
reader a place to breathe and a thesis to return to. Don't overuse it to the point
it goes soft.

**LOCKED — "Give me one more breath, and I'll breathe for both of us."** Your line,
unedited. Assessed as the strongest sentence in the manuscript. Nobody touches it.

**SETTLED — the refrain flips at the end.** Every prior appearance is an external
observation: love did something *to* you. The last time it appears, it turns into
something you're now doing. Not "that's what love does to me" — **"so that's what
I'm going to do."** Description becomes commission. That's John 21 in plain
language, no citation required.

# IX. THE ENDING

Two rounds went into this and it landed here.

**SETTLED — what it cannot be.** Not "I was healed and everything was great." Not
seven steps to victory. Not a claim that you're recovered or stable in any final
sense. You've been too insistent about not overselling grace as a free pass to let
the last chapter contradict it.

**SETTLED — what the last chapter actually is.** Every other chapter ends at a
bottom or a rescue. This one ends **at the desk** — you, present tense, writing this
manuscript, as the current form the mission takes. *I am still being met at the end
of myself, and this time the end of myself looked like a blank page.*

So the final chapter isn't a resolution to your story. It's the moment your story
turns and starts reaching for the reader's hand the same way His hand reached for
yours.

**SETTLED — the closing sequence, in order:**

1. **The refrain, flipped** — commission, not description.
2. **Direct address to the reader at their own bottom.** The whole book has looked
   backward. The last page turns and looks forward. *I'm not writing to the version
   of you that has it together. I never had it together either.*
3. **The Friday teaching, transcribed — not summarized.** This is a hard ruling. The
   welcome (dope fiends, convicts, the atheists who had God used as a weapon against
   them, the abused and forgotten) and the fire story and the question. Word for
   word, exactly as you preach it live, profanity intact or not depending on how far
   you want the raw voice to carry onto the page. It is not a passage to compress.
   It's already tested live every Friday. It just needs a spine of paper instead of
   a Facebook feed.
4. **The last words on the page: "Are you coming?"** Unanswered. Hanging in white
   space.
5. **Turn the page — and He's the one descending.**

**SETTLED — the Jesus-side finale.** The Great Commission (Matt 28:18-20, Acts 1:8).
He harrowed hell, freed the captives, rose — and immediately sent them back for the
ones still in the dark. Not "rest now, you're saved." *Go.* The mirror closes: He
was harrowed-out and sent back; you were harrowed-out and sent back. Both end on the
same question.

**Final line of the book: "Are you coming?"**

# X. THE PREQUEL

**SETTLED — "The Son of Man and the Curse of Fathers," in two movements.**

**0a — The Hidden One.** 1 Enoch 46-48. The Son of Man named *before the sun and the
constellations were created*, concealed with the Most High, chosen and hidden until
the appointed time. Function: He existed and was named before the world's
foundation — before your bloodline, before Abbeville, before any curse.

**0b — The Descent Through the Gates of Heaven.** *Ascension of Isaiah* 10-11,
preserved most completely in Ge'ez, flagged as ancient tradition. The Beloved
descends through seven heavens, changing His glory at each level, unrecognized by
the angels below, so He can pass undetected into the world and defeat the god of
that world.

Note what this does: it is Philippians 2:7 **dramatized as narrative** — the
stripping you already wrote about in Chapter Two, shown in motion before you explain
the theology of it. It is also the *corrected* kenosis in story form: veiled glory,
not subtracted deity. This prequel and the Chapter Two rewrite are the same fix
arriving from two directions.

**Paired against it: your father's and grandfather's history.** Documented, not
invented. The cycle framed as a small earthbound expression of the same rulers of
this age that Colossians 2:15 says Christ came to disarm. This is the strongest
placement in the whole book for the generational-curse material, because Christ is
already descending past hostile powers in the same chapter.

**SETTLED — if the family record is thin.** Say so plainly. Lean on the
Isaiah/Enoch framing and state that the specifics are lost but the pattern is not.
That's its own honest statement, not a gap to hide.

# XII. ONE EDITORIAL FLAG I'D BE DOING YOU A DISSERVICE TO LEAVE OUT

In the outlines for the post-salvation chapters, the drafting model wrote specific
methods into the scene as narrative furniture, and asked you to supply method
details for tone.

**Cut all of it, and don't supply it.** Two reasons, both practical:

*Publishing.* Every serious house and every serious ministry publisher screens
memoir against safe-messaging standards. Method-specific detail in a suicide
narrative is the single most reliable way to get a manuscript sent back or an
imprint to pass. This is not squeamishness; it's the standard.

*Your actual readership.* The people you wrote this for are the people in the pit —
that's the entire premise of the Friday welcome. A memoir naming methods reaches
exactly the reader most likely to be harmed by it. You wrote a book whose thesis is
that love climbs into the pit. Handing someone a map while you're down there
undercuts the whole argument.

**And here's the craft point: the scene is stronger without it.** The load-bearing
element was never the mechanism. It's *the end of myself* — the point where there
was nothing left, and something met you there, and it never once led with judgment.
That's the material. The specifics add nothing to it and cost you the reader you
most wanted to reach.

The version that works: the bottom named plainly, no method, and the refrain doing
the work — one more breath, and I'll breathe for both of us.
