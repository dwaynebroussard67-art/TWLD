# Interlude

*[Draft frame. Not yet the author's words. The Note below the line is his, unchanged.]*

*A great while before day.*

It is still dark. A man gets up, goes out alone, finds a place where nobody is, and prays. Later the men who follow him have to go looking for him.

Mark wrote that morning down. *And in the morning, rising up a great while before day, he went out, and departed into a solitary place, and there prayed.*

Luke wrote down others. The wilderness. A mountain, all night.

They wrote down that he went. Nobody wrote down what he said out there.

But the night they came for him, he prayed where his friends could hear, and it got written down. He prayed for the men who were with him. Then he said this:

*Neither pray I for these alone, but for them also which shall believe on me through their word.*

Them also. The ones who weren't there. The ones who weren't born yet.

I wasn't born yet.

I won't tell you what the King prayed out there in the dark. I don't know. Nobody does. I can only tell you what came through the veil and found me. I wrote it down before it could get away.

**A Note from the King**  
*As received*

The devil has the world convinced they need Jesus.

Sounds scriptural, doesn't it? Religion teaches it. But let me catch you up.

To need something is to imply that you at some point did not have it.

And there has never been that moment. Not for one human being ever born. Every soul carries a spark of infinity, marked by Jesus, and not one of them has been alone for a single second. If an angel was watching over you, he was there. Himself. Because if the devil ever got one second, just one, with you unprotected by God, he would end your life.

You never needed him. You only needed to see that you had him.

Separation was never real. It was the only trick he had.

And the devil's list is short. Read it. *The thief comes only to steal, and to kill, and to destroy.* Three verbs. That's all he's got. But the verse doesn't stop there. *I came that they might have life, and have it to the full.*

So when scripture says, *count it all joy when you fall into diverse temptations, knowing that the testing of your faith works patience,* it sounds like God is telling you to be happy while you suffer. He isn't.

Let me say the other half. God does not allow anyone to be tested by being raped. God does not allow anyone to be tested by a child being hurt. If that's the God you were told about, that's not the one I know. I'm talking about Jesus Christ. Evil has consequences. Some curses run back thousands of years through bloodlines you'll never meet, and they've found you because nobody had the courage to face Jesus and fight them with him. You don't have to kill them. You just have to swing your arms.

The test is something else. A trial of your faith, and he is not the one giving it. He's the teacher. He already taught you what you'd need, and he won't let you face what he hasn't prepared you for, what he hasn't lived himself. The test comes from someone else, an outside proctor who is not on your side. Before Peter was sifted, Jesus had already prayed for him.

Jesus is always in the room. What changes is the role. When the trial of your faith begins, he moves from teacher to intercessor and protector, praying for you and standing between you and the proctor. When it's the devil's work against you, he's the savior, fighting to get you out. Which one he plays depends on how you got there.

A lot of the time it's us. We make terrible choices, then blame God for what we walked into ourselves. But some people are innocent. Job was blameless, and the fire still came. In those moments, he moves heaven and earth to protect that person as much as he can. And if you can't tell which one you're in, it doesn't matter. He's in the room either way.

Every time I met Jesus in the worst of my life, I was at the end of myself, ready to quit. And he came, often looking like a man on fire. He never spoke out loud. It was in my mind, every time, the same words:

*I know it seems like the end. I know how hard it is. I wanted to quit too. I cried out. Take one more step for me and I'll carry you. Draw one more breath and I'll breathe for both of us. It's okay that you're at the end of yourself. You were made that way.*

They told you what was done to you was God's doing, or God's allowing. It was not. What was done to you had nothing to do with God. Every second of it, he was there, begging you to look at him. Just look at me. Just look at me. When your mind left what was happening, that was him, taking your eyes off it. You were never alone. Not even then. He was fighting his hardest to protect you and to hold the damage down. He does not override free will, and evil has consequences that run farther than we understand. But he never caused it, and he never stopped fighting for you.

And if you're hearing this through your own wound, hear this last.

What was done to you was not good, and he never called it good. But he is not finished. *And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.* He takes what was meant to kill you and works every piece of it toward your good.

You were never alone in it. And you're not alone now. The King is alive, active, and present!
