# INTERLUDE: THE THIEF'S EYES

*Friday, and then Saturday, as Dismas saw them.*

---

## I. The Hill

Nobody on Golgotha knew my name, or cared to. The soldiers called us what we were: two men on two crosses, one on each side of the One they had put in the middle. The crowd had a word for us, and it wasn't kind. Later, the tradition gave me the name Dismas. I didn't have it then. I had a mouth full of curses and wood under my back.

For hours I cursed. I cursed the soldiers, the priests with their robes pulled tight, the crowd, the nails, and my own mother for having me. I cursed the Man in the middle most of all, because He had a sign over His head that said KING, and the people walking past were laughing at it. "If You're the Christ," I spat up at Him, "save Yourself. And us."

The man on my other side didn't say anything for a long time. Then he turned his head the way a man does when every breath is a fight, and he told me to shut my mouth.

"Don't you even fear God?" he rasped. "We're getting what we earned. This Man hasn't done one thing wrong."

I was furious at him. Furious that the other thief was the one who said the true thing. But I looked at the Man in the middle, and His face wasn't hard. He wasn't shouting down at us. He was looking out at the crowd the way a father looks at children throwing rocks, like He could see what they couldn't.

"Jesus," I said. It came out as a croak. "Remember me when You come into Your kingdom."

He turned His head toward me. It took Him a long time. He had to push Himself up on the nail through His feet to pull in enough air to speak, and I watched Him do it, and I will never forget what it cost Him to say the next thing.

"Truly I tell you," He said, "today you will be with Me in paradise."[1]

Today. I held onto that word the way I held onto the wood.

Then the soldiers came with their mallets. They broke the legs of the man on my left first. Then mine. The sound came before the pain did. My feet had been holding up my weight, and when they were gone, all of me hung from the nails in my wrists and the beam across my shoulders. I couldn't push up anymore. Every breath cost a fist. In less than a minute I learned what a cross really is: a slow way of drowning on dry land. Then the sky went black, and then it wasn't the sky anymore. It was the inside of my own skull.

The last thing I felt was a splinter of the crossbeam grinding into my back.

I didn't know yet that a piece of that wood had come with me.

---

## II. The Dark

When I woke up, I wasn't choking. I was standing.

There was no floor, only a grey expanse of ash that ran out until it disappeared. The air smelled of sulfur and old copper, and under that was a stale, heavy breath that I understood was the breath of everyone who had ever died without hope. Millions of shadows moved out there, huddled and chained, and I could hear them weeping in a way I can't describe.

Then the memories came back all at once, and they were clear, the way water is clear when it's cold. I remembered every coin I ever stole. The cheap wine, the fights in the market, the dust in my teeth. My mother's face when I was small. And underneath all of that, older than any of it, I remembered a room I had been in before I was born, full of light I have no words for. I knew I had been made for something. I had thrown it away a piece at a time.

And I knew, the way you know you're going to be sick a second before it happens, that I was dead.

I looked down at my body. The welts from the whip were gone. My legs were whole. My shoulders, which had been torn open, were just skin and muscle, and they didn't hurt. It was the first time in my life I had been in my body without something waiting to hurt it. I should have been glad. Instead my knees went out from under me.

*Why am I here?* I thought. *I didn't accept Him. I didn't do the rituals. I cursed Him until I could barely breathe, and only at the very end did I ask Him for anything. I don't deserve one more breath. This isn't Paradise. Paradise is somewhere else.*

Then I heard it again in my head, in His voice, the same way I had heard the nails go in: *Today, you will be with Me in paradise.*

I stayed on my knees and held onto that word.

Then He came out of the grey.

He didn't look like a man who had just come back from the dead, not the way you'd expect. His body was whole. But He hadn't wiped away what had been done to Him. The stripes on His back were still there, deep and purple. There was dried blood on His forehead in a crown of small cuts. His wrists were open where the iron had gone through. I believe He kept them on purpose. He wanted us to see them, so we would always know what that love cost.

He walked up to me, and this time I didn't look away.

On the grey ground near us lay a splinter of the cross, thick as a finger and stained dark at one end. I hadn't noticed it before. He picked it up. He pressed it against the wound in His side, where the Roman spear had gone in, and drew it back wet. Then He held the splinter up between us.

The wood burst into light. It was only for a second, white and blinding, and then it settled into a low, steady glow, like a coal that wouldn't go out.

He put it in my hand. "Put that in your pocket."

I did. My hand shook so badly I almost dropped it.

Then He placed both hands on my head and prayed over me. I can't remember all the words. I remember the heat of His palms going down through my skull into my chest. "Close your eyes," He said, "and turn in a circle. You'll feel a pull. That pull is My son. Follow it until you find him, and when you do, tell him his Father sent you."[2]

I turned in a circle with my eyes shut. It felt like being tugged by a rope tied somewhere under my ribs. When I stopped, the pull was sharper, and it ran in one direction, down into the dark.

"You'll reach the gates before I do," He said. "You'll see things that will try to break your heart with fear. Don't be afraid. If anything comes close, take out the wood, close your eyes, and keep walking. The light will be blinding. That blood will keep them from touching you."

I didn't ask Him any questions. I didn't ask for a guarantee. I just said, "Yes, Lord," and started walking.

---

## III. The Gates

The gates of Hades were black, and high, and shut. Two guardians stood in front of them, tall as the gates, made of black iron and shadow. When they saw me, they laughed, and the laughing was wet and heavy.

"Another thief," one of them said.

They raised their blades. I closed my eyes, pulled the splinter out of my pocket, and held it up.

The light came out of my hand like a blast of wind off the sea. The two guardians shrieked. I heard iron hit stone, then boots scraping as they ran, then nothing but the hum of the wood in my hand. I opened my eyes once, just a crack, and saw them flee into the gloom like dogs.

I went through the gates.

After that I walked for what felt like a long time, though I couldn't tell you how long. Whenever the dark got thick enough to choke on, I pulled the wood out for a breath and looked for the blackest, emptiest corridor ahead, the one the pull led toward. Then I put the light away and walked into the dark it pointed to. I passed people chained to walls, begging me for water I didn't have. I passed a pit where a king was gnawing on his own hands. I didn't stop. I had been told to follow the pull, and I did.

---

## IV. Adam

He was in the lowest cavern, sitting on the bare floor with his back against the stone.

I had never seen him, but I knew him the moment I did. He was a big man, broad in the shoulders, gone thin and grey with waiting. His hair hung in clumps. His hands were scarred from thousands of years of digging at the walls, and he had the stillness of a man who had learned that moving only brings more pain.

He saw the light before I reached the cavern. The wood's glow reached the wall ahead of me, and I watched his face go hard with terror.

He pressed himself back against the rock and held his breath. He didn't say a word. He was trying to make himself into a shadow. I think he believed the demons were playing their old games again, showing him the light he had lost.

The glow came closer. Then it was in front of him, and I was standing there with the splinter in my hand.

"Put that thing out!" he roared, shielding his eyes with both hands. "Put it out right now! What the hell are you doing down here? Who are you, and how are you standing here in a body? Nothing down here has a body!"

I shoved the wood back into my tunic, and the cavern dropped into dim shadow. I stood there with my hands open and my head bowed, the way I had seen people do in the temple, and I said the only thing I had.

"I'm sorry, sir. I didn't mean to frighten you. I was sent here to bring you a message."

He breathed hard in the dark. "From who?"

"From the Man who died next to me on the hill," I said. "He's coming for you. He told me to tell you that you're His son. And He said your Father sent Him."[3]

Adam didn't move. He stared at me, and the shadows in his face shifted. I don't know how long he stayed that way, and I can't tell you what he was feeling. I only know that when the ground started to shake, he was still on his knees, looking at me like I was the first real thing he had seen in a thousand years.

---

## V. The Breach

It was a sound like the world tearing in half.

The bronze gates went first, and then the iron doors behind them. The sound rolled through the whole pit, through the chains and the walls and the bones of everyone in there. The screaming around us changed pitch. I will hear that sound as long as I live: thousands of voices going quiet at once.

Then the light came in, and this time it wasn't a splinter. It was the Light itself, and it filled the cavern from wall to wall. I couldn't look at it. I covered my face with my arm, and I still saw red through my own skin.

The psalm came first, from far off, in a voice that wasn't loud but carried anyway, the way a bell carries across a valley:

"Lift up your heads, O ye gates; and be ye lift up, ye everlasting doors; and the King of glory shall come in."[4]

The guardians answered, and they were terrified. "Who is this King of glory?"

What came back was the voice that had told me *today*: "The LORD strong and mighty, the LORD mighty in battle."[4]

When I got my arm down, He was walking through the broken threshold of the cavern, and the dark was leaving the stones as He came. He didn't look at the demons crawling away from His path. He didn't look at the chains. He looked at the man on the floor.

Adam stood up. His legs shook so badly he had to put a hand on the wall. Then he went down on his knees in front of the King, his face wet, and he didn't say anything at all.

The King knelt down in front of him and held out His scarred hand, and waited.

Adam took it. He took it the way you'd take hold of something you had been afraid to touch for a long, long time.

The King raised him up. Then He turned to the rest of the pit, and the holy ones came out of the dark to meet Him: Abraham, Moses, David, the prophets, the kings who had kept faith in the shadows, and the ones whose names had been lost in the grey. They came in a long line. The King took each one by the hand as they passed, and led them toward the gate.

They walked past me. Nobody stopped. Nobody pushed.

I stayed back out of the way, with the splinter in my hand and my knees shaking.

When the last of the righteous had gone through, the King looked back across the cavern. His eyes moved through the souls still left in the dark, searching, the way a man searches a field for one lost sheep. Then He found me. He didn't say anything. He just looked at me, and I understood He had been looking for me the whole time.

He walked back across the broken ground and stopped in front of me. He looked down at my hands, filthy and empty and shaking. Then He took my wrist in a grip like solid iron and drew me toward the gate.

At the threshold He stopped and turned me to face Him.

"Are you coming?" He asked.

I looked up at Him, at the scars and the blood and the light. I thought about every lie I had ever told, every coin I had taken, every curse I had thrown at Him on the hill. I had nothing to offer Him. I had only ever been a thief.

"I'm coming," I said.

He smiled, a tired smile, the kind a man gives when he has finished a hard job and sees the next one waiting. Then He walked me through the gate and into the light. Behind us, the dark of Hades was already closing, and the pit where the chains had been stood empty.

I was the last one through the gate. And the first thing I saw on the other side was the King of glory, standing and waiting for me.

**Notes**

[1] Luke 23:39–43. The "today" promise is the anchor for the whole interlude.

[2] Luke 3:38 — Adam is called "the son of God." The "pull" and the blessing are the author's additions and are not in any text.

[3] Luke 3:38, as above.

[4] Psalm 24:7–8. The gate cry and the guardians' question both come from the psalm. The answer, "The LORD strong and mighty," is Psalm 24:8.

[5] John 19:32–34 — the legs of both thieves are broken; Jesus is already dead when the spear enters His side. The splinter is the author's addition.

*Ancient tradition, not canonical.* The Harrowing scenes (the gates broken open, the psalm exchange with the guardians, the patriarchs led out, the thief brought out of Hades) follow the Gospel of Nicodemus (Descensus ad Inferos), flagged per the sourcing rule in TWLD-DECISIONS §II. It is not part of the Ethiopian canon.
