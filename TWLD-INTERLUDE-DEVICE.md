# THAT'S WHAT LOVE DOES — The Interlude Device

**Date:** 2026-11-10. Proposed. The author asked for the device; the frame wording is the editor's draft until he rules.
**First use:** `manuscript/interludes/01-there-was-never-separation.md`
**The brief, in the author's words** (full dictation in `TWLD-SPOKEN-2026-11-10.md`):

> So I would like to work out something where, like, there was like an interlude or something between chapters where, you know, a strange figure who we obviously know is Jesus, but we don't really explicitly say it. We can just say the king or whatever. In his quiet contemplations, reaches forward through the veil to find me, because this is a personal story, and to download important information to me that he knew this world needed.

> Now, I know we have to be careful with that. I know it has to be handled just right.

---

## 1. What it had to solve

Two things the author has said both have to hold.

- He wants the King shown reaching him directly, between chapters.
- His locked rule (TWLD-DECISIONS §I) says never claim what He felt, and no words in His mouth or His head.

A scene of the King thinking a note toward the future breaks the second one. So the device invents nothing about him.

## 2. Where the interludes live: the prayers nobody wrote down

The Gospels say again and again that he went off alone to pray. They almost never say what he prayed. That silence is on the record. It is the same move the book already makes with the silent years (TWLD-DECISIONS §III): do not fill the gap, let the gap be the point.

Every interlude opens in one of those recorded moments. The page says that he went. It does not say what he said. Then the Note sits on the other side of that silence, and the reader connects the two without being told how.

Because nothing about him is invented, the frame stays inside Tier 1 and Tier 3 of TWLD-CHRIST-SIDE-RULE.md. It needs no "imaginative interlude" label, and it does not have to wait on the tier ruling.

## 3. The three beats

1. **The King's side.** One recorded time he went off to pray. No name: "a man," "he," then "the King." Only what the verse says he did.
2. **The crossing.** One spoken line, the same in every interlude, so a listener learns the signal. Proposed: *I can only tell you what came through the veil and found me. I wrote it down before it could get away.* "The veil" is the author's image. "Before it could get away" is from the introduction to his Notes collection.
3. **The Note.** The author's words under his own heading, *A Note from the King / As received.* Verbatim. The frame can be rewritten or cut without touching it.

## 4. The test for a Note: the dark mirror

The author, 2026-11-10: "for every note from the king, there's usually a dark mirror." "There's always some thing that I perceive in the world that the king sees opposite to where the way the world sees it." "The notes are his correcting my assumptions about things I thought I knew."

Before a Note is placed between chapters, name its mirror in two lines, using the Note's own words. Nothing extra is printed; the Notes already open on their mirror.

- **Interlude I.** The world: "The devil has the world convinced they need Jesus." The King: "You never needed him. You only needed to see that you had him."
- **Existing Note II, for comparison.** The world: "Addict. Sinner. Repeat offender." The King: "I look at the same room and I see soldiers."

A piece that has no mirror may be a reflection rather than a Note, and may sit better in the companion.

## 5. The well: recorded times he went to pray

Frames draw from these, roughly in this order. The run ends in the present tense, which is where the first Note ends: "The King is alive, active, and present!"

1. Mark 1:35. "Rising up a great while before day," a solitary place. *Used in Interlude I.*
2. Luke 5:16. He "withdrew himself into the wilderness, and prayed."
3. Luke 6:12. A mountain. He "continued all night in prayer to God."
4. Matthew 14:23. A mountain apart; "when the evening was come, he was there alone."
5. Luke 9:18. "As he was alone praying."
6. Luke 11:1. "Praying in a certain place." They watched, then asked to be taught.
7. Luke 22:32. "But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not." A prayer already prayed that nobody heard. The first Note points at this one.
8. Luke 22:41. A stone's cast away, kneeling.
9. Hebrews 5:7. "Prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears."
10. Hebrews 7:25. "He ever liveth to make intercession for them."

Where scripture does give his words (John 17; the garden), quote them and add nothing.

## 6. Rules for writing a frame

- Never name him. Never say what he prayed, thought, or felt.
- Quote the King James Version. It is public domain in the United States, and the first Note already quotes Romans 8:28 from it word for word.
- Never present the Note as his dictated words. The Notes speak in the author's voice about him. The frame says only that something came through and the author wrote it down.
- Keep it short. The first frame runs about 200 words because it sets the pattern. Later frames need three or four sentences: where he went, that nobody wrote down what he said, the crossing line.
- It has to work read aloud. The crossing is a spoken line, not an ornament.

## 7. Why the first frame ends on John 17:20

On the one night his prayer was written down at length, he prayed for people who had not been born: "Neither pray I for these alone, but for them also which shall believe on me through their word." That is the first Note's claim, that there was never a moment without him, shown before the Note says it.
