## 1. THE CORE CONCEPT

A parallel-biography memoir: one chapter follows **your** life, the next follows **Jesus's** life, at the same age. The two threads run separately almost the entire book — "because he was perfect and I wasn't, not even close" — and converge at the end in a jail cell.

**Purpose of the pairing (in your words):** *"to create a contrast between what a perfect life looks like and what a non-perfect life looks like. It's to hold a mirror up to everybody else."*

**The central theological claim:** Western Christianity celebrates Sunday (Resurrection) as the victory. This book argues Saturday — the **Harrowing of Hell**, when Jesus's spirit descended to Hades between His death and resurrection and broke the gates from the inside to free the captives — was the real conquest. The book's climax reframes this: instead of (or alongside) rescuing Adam, Christ descends into *your* hell and gets *you*. Not a past event — ongoing. *"He came for Adam 2,000 years ago. He came for me yesterday. He came for me today. He's coming for me tomorrow. The Harrowing didn't end on Saturday. It's still happening."*

**The recurring refrain / thesis line, used as a chorus throughout:**
> "...and that's what love does."

It appears at the end of scenes of rescue, descent, and repeated forgiveness — doing real structural work as a place for the reader to breathe and a thesis to return to.

**Key numerical parallel driving the structure:** Jesus died at 33. You were saved at 33. The timelines are built to converge at that age.

## 2. THE TWO KEY TERMS

- **Khuba** — the Aramaic word for love used as the book's definition of love: not a feeling, but "a debt of the soul that moves before you decide to move" — active, sacrificial, one-way, no exit ramp. This is the term that dismantles what the book calls the "Hallmark version" of love.
- **Nura** — the new life/light Christ brings into a dark space. (Flagged in the conversation as needing one clear, consistent definition early on, since Khuba and Nura get used close together and could otherwise read as inconsistent rather than a deliberate pairing.)

## 4. GOVERNING RULES YOU SET FOR THE BOOK

These are constraints you stated directly, and they should override anything else in this document if they conflict:

- **It is not a memoir.** It's presented as the story of Jesus — specifically the "silent years" you feel Western tradition skips — with your life held up as the mirror against it.
- **Feeling over action.** *"People never understand what happens. They understand how it feels... concentrate heavily on the feeling more than the action of the stuff that's happening."*
- **Hard limit on Christ's interior life:** never invent or assume what Jesus felt. You can only relate your own human experience to what a moment would have felt like from a human vantage point — never put words in His head.
- **No shadow on Christ.** Any inference about Jesus's silent years must be plausible, defensible, and theologically safe — never anything that reflects negatively on Him, and never a stretch beyond what's reasonably arguable.
- **Stay close to the evidence.** Inference is necessary to cover 33 years of silence, but it should never feel invented — it should always read as "this could have happened," not fabrication.

## 5. SOURCING FRAMEWORK — what counts as what

This got corrected mid-conversation and is worth locking in before you write further, since the first version overstated what's actually in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo canon.

**Actually Scripture-tier (Ethiopian Orthodox canon, 81 books — broader than the 66/73-book Western canons):**
- The four Gospels, Acts, Epistles, Revelation (same content as Western Bibles)
- **1 Enoch** — Son of Man prophecies, pre-existence of the Messiah, spiritual warfare framework. Quoted directly in Jude 1:14–15.
- **Jubilees** — expanded Genesis/Exodus chronology and angelology
- 1, 2, 3 Meqabyan (a different set of books than the Western "Maccabees," despite the name)
- Sinodos, Didascalia, Book of the Covenant, Ethiopic Clement — but these are church-order/liturgy texts, **not narrative material about Jesus's life**

**Important correction:** none of the above covers Jesus's boyhood or the "silent years" (ages 12–30). There is no ancient text, Ethiopian or otherwise, that narrates that gap — which is precisely why it's called "silent."

**Real, useful, but must be labeled "ancient tradition," not Scripture:**
- **Gospel of Nicodemus (Acts of Pilate)** — the single best source for the detailed Harrowing-of-Hell scene: Christ confronting Hades and Satan by name, breaking the bronze gates, taking Adam's hand, leading the captives out. This is the backbone of your climax.
- Infancy Gospel of Thomas, Protevangelium of James, Arabic/Ethiopic Infancy Gospel — cover ages roughly 0–12 only (clay-bird miracle, Joseph's board, the Temple scene at 12). Devotional tradition, never canonized by any church including Ethiopia's.
- The **Anastasis icon** (standard Eastern Orthodox Resurrection image for 1,000+ years) — shows Christ standing on the broken gates of Hades gripping Adam's wrist. Worth citing directly: your climax is you personalizing this exact, ancient image.

**Do not use — flagged as fabricated/unreliable, not ancient tradition at all:**
- Gnostic gospels (Thomas [the Gnostic one], Philip, Mary)
- The *Aquarian Gospel* and the Notovitch "Jesus in India/Tibet" story — both 19th/20th-century inventions; the Notovitch manuscript has been shown never to have existed as claimed.

**Scripture that already supports the Harrowing doctrine directly, without needing any extra-canonical material:** 1 Peter 3:18–20 and 4:6, Ephesians 4:8–10, Acts 2:27/Psalm 16:10, Colossians 2:15, Revelation 1:18, Matthew 27:52–53 — plus the Apostles' Creed line "He descended into hell," which most Western churches already recite without ever preaching on it.

## 6. THE CHAPTER TWO THEOLOGICAL CORRECTION (settled 08/02/2026)

The original draft of Chapter Two said Jesus "took off His divinity" and became fully man, so divested that at the cross He could only beg, not command. This doesn't hold — it contradicts Philippians 2:7 (your own footnote) and Luke 23:43 ("Today you will be with me in Paradise," spoken after the point the chapter says He'd laid it all down).

**The corrected footing:**
> He didn't surrender His divinity. He surrendered the *use* of it. He kept everything — the knowing, the identity, Alpha and Omega intact — and united it to a human nature that could bleed and stop and die. Then He refused, as God, with all of it still in His hand, to call the nails off. That's how you kill God: not by making Him killable, but by joining Him to flesh that was, and watching Him decline to save Himself.

This is stronger for your Harrowing chapter too: the One who goes down into Hell has to still *be* God when He arrives, or the gates don't come off.

## 7. STRUCTURAL OUTLINE — most developed version

- **0a — The Hidden One:** 1 Enoch 46–48 (genuine Scripture-tier). The Son of Man existed before creation, hidden with the Father, chosen to judge and break chains. Establishes Christ's mission as planned invasion, not accident.
- **0b — The Descent Through the Gates of Heaven:** Ascension of Isaiah 10–11 (ancient tradition, flagged as such) — the Beloved descends through seven heavens, changing glory at each level, unrecognized, to defeat "the god of that world." A narrative dramatization of the Philippians 2:7 "stripping" you already wrote in Chapter Two.
- **The Curse of Fathers:** your father's and grandfather's history — documented where possible, not invented — framing the generational cycle of violence (Exodus 34:7) as an earthbound expression of the same powers Colossians 2:15 says Christ came to disarm.
