# NOTES FROM THE KING

*Seventeen notes. Dressed for reading. Nothing here is theory — every one of these came down in a truck cab, a driveway, or a dark room in Abbeville, and got written down before it could get away.*

---

## I. You Will Never Fit In

Let me clear something up before you go any further.

We are not telling you that if you come to the King, you'll finally start fitting in. That's a lie people sell to get you in a building.

The opposite is true. You'll be more of a misfit than you have ever been. The world had a slot for you when you were carrying its weight. The second you put that weight down, the slot closes.

You will fit with God. You will fit with us. You will not fit with the world, and you should stop expecting to.

And understand why. It isn't that we're exclusive. It's that they are. They shut the door on the truth a long time ago, and we're just standing on the other side of it.

---

## II. There Are Warriors in That Room

I know what the world calls you. Addict. Sinner. Repeat offender.

I look at the same room and I see soldiers.

It takes a savage kind of strength to go eight and nine rounds with a thing that is actively trying to bury you and still wake up. Most people in this city could not carry a week of what you carry every day — the shame, the sorrow, the weight of it. You've been fighting hard. Nobody can say you haven't.

You've just been fighting for a god that's killing you. You gave it your money, your family, your years, your blood. You've been the most loyal soldier that thing ever had.

Jesus said, *come to me, all you who are weary and heavy laden, and I will give you rest.* He is not asking you to fight harder. He is telling you to put the gear down. Take my yoke, He says, because it's easy and the burden is light.

You're still a warrior. You're just changing generals.

---

## III. He Is Not Intimidated By Your Choices

Maybe you're reading this with a bowl rolling. Maybe there's a needle in reach.

He is not backing up.

He isn't standing there disgusted, waiting for you to become presentable. That's a picture religion painted, and it's wrong. He's got His arms open right now, in this exact moment, in the state you're actually in.

He says: *I'll love you exactly like you are, if you let me.*

You don't have to change first. You don't have to become a good person first. You don't have to beat the thing on your back before you're allowed to come — because you can't, and He knows you can't, and that was never the arrangement.

His love does the burning. His presence does the work. And one day you'll notice the thing that owned you isn't there anymore, and you won't even be able to say when it left.

You'll just need Him.

---

## IV. The Name and the Word

Here's one worth sitting with.

Anywhere in this book, in this ministry, in your Bible — take the word *love*, the real kind, the sacrificial kind, Khuba — and put His name in its place. Then read it again.

It doesn't change. Not one line of it. The meaning holds perfectly, every time.

Love is patient. Love is kind. Love keeps no record of wrongs. Love never fails.

He is the definition. He isn't a thing that has love. He is the word itself, walking around in a body.

Which means if you say you're full of love but you don't know the King, you're talking about a shadow of the thing.

---

## V. Love Changes Its Existence

I've had my heart broken more times than I can count, and almost every time it came with the same sentence: *You're too good for me. You deserve somebody better.*

That is the coward's exit. They take from a giver until the giver finally needs something back, and the second the debt shows up, they dress up leaving as a favor to you.

That is not what love does.

In every relationship I've had, I changed my own shape trying to be what they needed. I shaved pieces off myself to fit the gaps they had. I never got it right, but that was the direction love pushed me in — toward becoming what the other person needed.

Now look at the King.

He didn't look down into the pit at what we'd become and say *he deserves a better savior than me.* He didn't say we were too far gone to relate to. He changed His own existence. He put on flesh, took on hunger and grief and a body that could be broken, and stepped into the dirt to be what we needed before we knew how to ask.

Love doesn't leave to get out of the debt. Love changes what it is to come find you.

And that's what love does.

---

## VI. Love Is Right Now

This one was hard for me to accept and it's true anyway.

If you love who I used to be, you don't love me. If you love who I could become, you don't love me. If you love the version of me you've built in your head, you don't love me.

Love is a present-tense thing or it isn't love. Everything else is a photograph or a wish.

Now turn that around on Him, because that's where it gets heavy.

Jesus does not love the innocent kid you were before the world got to you. He does not love the cleaned-up person you might be in ten years, the one who has it together and teaches on Sundays. He does not stand outside the room waiting for that version to show up.

He loves the junkie. He loves the crackhead. He loves the man sitting in the fire right now.

Not the ghost. Not the potential. You. Today. As you actually are.

---

## VII. What Loyalty Actually Is

I have a brother. He's married, and I love his wife — she's my sister. And you will never once hear about me spending time alone with that woman outside his presence. Not because anybody suspects anything. Because I refuse to hand the enemy a single seed to plant in my brother's heart.

Most people use loyalty as a leash. *I'll do whatever I want, and if you're loyal you'll trust me.*

That's backwards.

Real loyalty means I will never put you in a position where you have to wonder about me. I don't make you choose to trust me. I move in a way that makes trust the only thing that makes sense.

And in any situation where our interests touch, here's what I'm doing: whatever is best for you. Not for me. Every time, for the rest of my life.

The day you have to wonder what I'm doing is the day my loyalty deserves to be questioned.

True loyalty never makes you wonder how I move.

---

## VIII. The Seed That Doesn't Divide

Ask yourself a simple question. What is infinity divided by infinity?

It's infinity. You can cut it forever and every piece is still endless. That's what makes it what it is.

Now hold that next to this. The difference between us and God was never that He's good and we're garbage. In the garden, Adam looked like Him, moved like Him, sounded like Him. The one real difference was that God is infinite and Adam wasn't.

So if He put a piece of Himself inside you — and He did, before you took a breath — then what's living in you isn't a fraction. There's no such thing as a fraction of infinity.

That's why He said a mustard seed of faith moves a mountain. It was never about the size of the seed. It's about what the seed is made of.

You're a finite man carrying an infinite God. That's the thing the enemy does not want you to work out.

---

## IX. The Burying of the Seed

Once you see that, you can see what he's actually been doing your whole life.

He isn't trying to kill you. He's trying to bury the seed so deep under mud that you forget it's down there.

He piles on distraction — things that don't matter, dressed up like things that do. Then despair, which is just the lie that *this is simply who I am.* Then religion, rules without any Spirit in them, which hardens over the top of everything like a shell.

Adam and Eve did the same thing on a smaller scale. They didn't just feel bad. They covered themselves and hid. And God walked through the garden asking *where are you* — not because He'd lost them, but because they'd buried themselves.

The enemy is a thief who dumps trash over a gold mine so nobody ever thinks to dig.

Jesus dug. That's what the Harrowing was. He went down into the deepest place there is and came back up with what was buried.

He wasn't digging for a better version of you. He was digging for Himself in you.

---

## X. The Broken Stones

Some days I stand up to speak and I think, *I'm not good enough to be the one saying this.* Bad grammar. Bad record. Bad man, most of my life. Why me?

The old teachers had a tradition about the tablets.

Moses came down the mountain with the law cut in stone by God's own hand, saw what the people were doing, and threw them down. They shattered. So God gave him a second set.

But the tradition says the broken pieces weren't swept up and thrown out. They went into the Ark. The shattered ones were laid in the same holy box as the whole ones, and God spoke from above that box for generations.

Sit with that. The seat of His voice on earth was built on top of broken pieces.

If I were polished, the throwaways would smell the plastic on me and never come near. They come because they see a man dragging a shattered life behind him telling them the line is open.

The cracks aren't the failure. The cracks are how the light gets out.

---

## XI. The Right Now Heart

When I came out of prison I gave God a lot. My worship. My gratitude. My respect. My time. I thought I'd handed Him my life.

I'd held back two things and didn't even know it.

The first was my right-now heart. Not some future, repaired, presentable heart — this one, the human one, in the shape it's in today. I'd been saving that for a person. For a wife. For a family that hadn't shown up yet.

The second was my future. I wanted to do good things, sure. But I wanted to do them and I wanted the credit. I never handed Him the plans.

Here's the difference between then and now: the fire.

I knew about Him before. I didn't know His suffering. I didn't understand what it costs to be rebuilt in His image — which is mostly this: loving people who will not love you back.

That's Khuba. That's making intercession for the people driving the nails.

---

## XII. Baptism Is a Funeral

Most people who get baptized have no idea what just happened to them. They know they got wet. That's it. They dry off and every ghost they walked in with walks back out.

The church took one of the heaviest things in the Kingdom and turned it into a polite bath.

Before John ever stood in the Jordan, the Hebrews had the mikvah — full immersion in living water, moving water, never a stagnant puddle. And it wasn't about dirt on skin. It was a grave and a womb at the same time. You go under and the old identity stops breathing. You come up taking a first breath as something that didn't exist ten seconds ago.

John's baptism added repentance, and repentance isn't an apology. It's a military about-face. It's a soldier realizing he's been marching for the wrong side, stopping in the mud, and turning all the way around in front of everybody.

Then John said the part everybody skips: *I baptize with water. The One coming after me will baptize with the Holy Spirit and with fire.*

Water can only reach the outside of a man. It can bury the old one. It cannot start the new one. The fire does that.

Without the fire, you're just a wet corpse.

If people understood it was a funeral, half of them would stay out of the water. The other half would come out warriors.

---

## XIII. No Longer I

I'm not a Sunday warrior. I'm not somebody who shows up once a week, treats God like a chore, and forgets the whole thing by Tuesday.

I'm past being a believer, too — believers add a Wednesday class and call it depth.

I'm not even "sold out." Sold out is a man who loves God all the way up to the moment the cross gets heavy.

Here's where I've landed. I don't want to live my life anymore. Not because I want out of it — because I've seen what I make of it when I'm driving. I've had the wheel and I know exactly what I do with it.

So I gave it to Him. Not my Sundays. The whole thing.

*I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me.*

That's not poetry. That's an eviction. The old man doesn't get renovated, he gets buried, and Somebody else moves into the house.

And a man who has genuinely handed over the deed is the hardest thing in the world to threaten. You can't bribe him with what he already gave away.

---

## XIV. Heaven Right Here

We pray it constantly and we never hear it. *Your kingdom come. Your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven.*

The answer is sitting in the second half of the sentence.

What's happening in heaven right now? God is being glorified. Constantly. Nothing up there is corrupted, so nothing up there withholds it.

So how do you get heaven onto a street in Abbeville? Same way. You glorify Him here.

And what glorifies Him is not a louder worship set. It's bending down to pick up your brother. It's feeding somebody who can't pay you back. It's kindness with no angle in it, no manipulation under it, no invoice coming later — just *you're a human being and God loves us, so I'm going to love you.*

That's the moment heaven touches concrete.

Stop waiting to die to see the Kingdom. Go drag some of it down here today.

---

## XV. One Blood

Race is a line men drew, and the enemy has been using it ever since to keep us swinging at each other instead of at him.

Understand how useful that is to him. As long as I can look at a man and find a reason he's beneath me, I never have to look at him and see a brother — and I never have to look at Christ and see a Savior who came out of a Middle Eastern people, not a European one, and who looked nothing like the picture hanging in most churches in this country.

Scripture says He made of one blood every nation of men.

One blood. That's the whole science and the whole theology in three words.

Here's the part that turns it from a rule into a wonder. God is infinite, and an infinite God expresses Himself infinitely. Of course we came out different. That was never the accident. That was the point. Every face you've ever seen is one more expression of a God who doesn't run out of ways to show Himself.

We are all different. We are all the same. Both are true, and the enemy needs you to only ever believe one of them.

---

## XVI. The Safekeeper of the Light

The light comes from the Father. It passes through the man as a seed. It is placed, under the guiding of the Holy Spirit, into the woman.

And then something happens that nobody should ever talk small about.

For nine months she is the only thing standing between that light and the cold. The DNA gets woven. The lungs get built. The heart starts. All of it happens inside her, on her blood and her bone and her strength — and then she keeps guarding it long after the nine months are up.

To shrink her half is to shrink the whole process God chose.

If you want to know what He thinks of it, look at where He put His own Son. Not a throne. Not a temple made of stone. The womb of a woman — the only place He judged holy enough to hold the Light of all creation while it learned how to be human.

And know this too: the enemy knows exactly how much weight she carries, which is why he comes at a house through her. Her vigilance is not a nice quality. It's a post. When she stands aligned with her husband and with the King, the thing that came looking for the weak point walks straight into the strongest one.

---

## XVII. Are You Coming?

When Jesus found me, I wasn't near the fire. I was hanging over the edge of it. The heat was baking my skin and the smoke was already in my lungs and I was a breath away from going in.

He reached into the flames and pulled me out.

The second my feet hit solid ground I dropped flat on my face. I was still smoking. I looked up at Him and I begged: *Lord, what can I do? How do I pay You back for dragging me out of that? How do I serve You?*

He didn't ask for a song. He didn't ask for a building.

He pointed back at the smoke and said, **"There are still people down there. I'm going back for them. Are you coming?"**

I said I'm coming.

He is still going back into that fire. I'm going with Him.

Are you coming?

---

## XVIII. The Name Is Not a Rank

There's a thing that happens to people when they get saved. Or what they call saved.

Two things, really.

The first is they want Jesus to be their Savior without being their Lord. They want the rescue. They don't want the ownership. They'll take the hand that pulls them out of the water and then argue about who's steering the boat.

The second is quieter, and it does more damage. They start believing the name makes them something. A rank. A higher shelf than the people who don't have it.

Let me cut that down.

If the name of Jesus is the reason you believe you're better than somebody, you missed the whole point of why He came. God forbid I ever use His name to believe I'm above anybody.

Here's what Love taught me. None of us are like Him. He was the only one good enough. Everything I bring to Him is a filthy rag next to what He is — and so is everything you bring. That's not an insult. That's the floor we're all standing on, and it's level.

So when I look at a man who doesn't believe what I believe and I decide I'm worth more than he is, that makes me a hypocrite. It doesn't make me a Christian.

And about that word.

Christian started out as an insult. That isn't a footnote, that's the whole history. At the time most people didn't believe Jesus was the Messiah, so when they saw the ones who did, they mocked them for it. *Look at them. Trying to be like Christ.* It was a word you used to put somebody underneath you.

Then the people it was thrown at picked it up and kept it. They answered to it on the way to being killed for it. That's how it stopped being an insult — not because anybody promoted it, but because men and women died wearing it.

So it was never a rank. It was a death sentence people accepted on purpose.

If you think getting saved put you above somebody, go read your book again. You missed it.

---

## XIX. What I Taught My Demons

I saw an image a while back that described me better than I could have written it myself. A man standing back to back with a demon — except the demon isn't separate from him, it's like a reflection stepping straight out of his own spine. They're both looking at each other over their shoulders. The caption read: *I didn't let go of my demons. I just taught them to be quiet. Now they laugh, quietly, at everybody who comes banging on the lock to their cage.*

That's me. Most people who know me now don't remember the man I was before prison.

I want to be clear about something, because it matters. I was never someone who went looking for a reason to hurt people. That was never who I was. But if you crossed me, back then, retribution didn't belong to God. It belonged to me. I handled it myself.

The difference in the man I am now isn't that the capacity disappeared. It's that I finally trust Him with it.

*Vengeance is mine, I will repay, says the Lord* — I used to read that verse and agree with it in theory while keeping the actual authority in my own hands. Now I mean it. Whatever's owed to the people who've hurt me, I've handed the collecting of it to Someone who's better at justice than I ever was, and a lot more patient than I ever could be.

The demon is still locked up back there. It's just not mine to let out anymore.
